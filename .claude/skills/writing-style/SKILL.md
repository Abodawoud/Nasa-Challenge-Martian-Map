# How we write

Every document, every review pack, every Slack message. Read this before you write, and check
against it before you finish.

We write for a reader whose English is good but not native. B1 level. Clear, warm, direct. It
should read like a careful colleague wrote it, not a machine.

## The rules

**Short sentences.** One idea each. Under 20 words is a good sign. If a sentence needs a
breath in the middle, split it.

**Common words.** Say "use", not "utilise". "Start", not "initiate". "Help", not "facilitate".
"Show", not "surface". "Need", not "require".

**Active voice.** "The user clicks Save", not "Save is clicked by the user".

**No em dashes and no en dashes.** Not the long dash (—) and not the short one (–). Use a
comma, a full stop, or brackets instead.

**No semicolons.** Make two sentences.

**No Latin and no abbreviations.** Do not write "e.g.", "i.e.", "etc.", "vs.", or "N/A". Write
"for example", "that is", "and so on", "against", "not applicable".

**Explain a technical word the first time.** Keep it only when it is the real name of a thing
(a file, a tool, a product, a persona). Then say what it means, once.

**Numbers as digits.** 3 seconds, not three seconds. Dates as 2026-09-08.

**Talk to the reader.** "You" and "we" where they fit. "We do not know yet", not "this remains
to be determined".

**No padding.** Not "in order to" (say "to"). Not "it should be noted that". Not "please
note". Not "basically", "essentially", "actually".

**One paragraph, one point.** When you compare things, a short table beats a long paragraph.

**Plain headings.** A noun or a short phrase. No colons, no questions.

**Say what is not known.** "Not decided yet, see D5" is a good sentence. A confident guess is
not.

## Before you finish

Read the whole document again, as the reader.

1. Count the long dashes. It must be 0.
2. Count the semicolons. It must be 0.
3. Find the longest sentence. Under 30 words, or split it.
4. Every technical word explained once, or it is the name of a thing.
5. Any sentence that sounds like a machine wrote it: rewrite it the way you would say it out
   loud.

IDs, tables, code blocks, and the five-line assumption shape are not prose. Leave those as
they are.
